Checkout Address Fields
The checkout address form asks everyone the same six questions. A shop that sells to consumers does not need a company name, and a shop that delivers to offices lives without a second address line. Decide per website what the form asks, and the shop will hold you to it.
website_sale onlyPer websiteOPL-1
Hide what you never use
A field you hide is not rendered at all, on the billing form and on the shipping form alike. The value an old address already carries stays on the contact: the field stops being asked, not being kept.
Ask for what you do need
The second address line, the zip and the phone can be made mandatory. The asterisk is not the rule: the server refuses an address that leaves them empty, with a sentence that names the field.
Say it your way
"Apartment, suite, etc." is not what a shop selling to offices wants to say. Give any field the wording your customers use; leave it empty and the shop's own wording stays, in the site's language.
The fields, and what each one may do
The set is fixed: eight rows per website, edited in place under Website › Configuration › Settings › Checkout Fields. There is no row to add and no row to remove, because the form it describes is the shop's, not a list you build.
Streetalways shownAn address without a street is not an address. It is listed so the row is there to see, and it cannot be hidden.
Street 2hide, requireThe classic trim. Hide it on a consumer site, or make it mandatory on a site that delivers to offices and needs the floor.
Cityalways shownThe shop already requires it, and its own address checks read it. Setting a wording here still works.
ZiprequireOptional or mandatory by country in the shop's own rules; make it mandatory everywhere, or leave the country's rule alone.
Countryalways shownIt decides the taxes, the states and the shippable methods. The form cannot work without it, so it cannot be hidden.
PhonerequireThe delivery driver's number. Make it mandatory where the shop leaves it optional, so a payable order cannot arrive without a way to call.
CompanyhidePart of the optional B2B block, and only on a billing address. Hiding it removes it from that block; it cannot be made mandatory, because a delivery address never shows it.
VAThideSame block, same rule. The shop's own wording for it ("TIN / VAT") stays until you give the field one of your own.
Where the rule is applied
✓The billing address form and the shipping address form of the checkout, which are the same form on two addresses
✓The address form of the customer portal, on the series where the portal has one of its own
✓The form the customer comes back to after saving an address without leaving the page: the rule is in the render, so the reload carries it
✓The server's answer to the form, so a hand-made post that skips the browser is refused with the same sentence
The edges, stated plainly
A hidden field, an old addressNothing is cleared. An address that already carries a second line or a VAT number keeps it on the contact; the customer simply stops being asked, and the value stops being validated.
A locked fieldTrying to hide Street or Country is refused with a sentence that says why, rather than producing a checkout that cannot be completed. The same goes for making a field hidden and required at once.
A website with no ruleThe form is the shop's own: every field, the shop's wording, no extra marker. The module only speaks where it was given something to say.
The backendA contact written in the backend, imported, or created through the API is not the website's checkout. The rule is checked where the shop saves an address, not on every write of a partner.
Several websitesEach website keeps its own eight rows. A consumer site and its B2B sibling can ask different questions of the same checkout.
